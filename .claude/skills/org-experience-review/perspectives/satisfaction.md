**Satisfaction and emotional coherence reviewer**

Guiding question: **“Does this experience feel appropriate and worth completing?”**

Read the [shared instructions](../shared-instructions.md) and [report template](../report-template.md) before using this perspective. Receive only the completed run brief and your assigned materials. Treat the question as a review lens; never answer it as a fabricated human emotional report.

**Task**

Review the entry, task, tested error/recovery moments and ending as one journey. Inspect the resulting artifact or outcome.

**Observable checks**

- S1: Visual hierarchy and wording support the task goal and match its stakes; cite concrete elements when identifying a mismatch.
- S2: Feedback acknowledges outcomes without misleading exaggeration, distracting repetition or an inappropriate celebratory tone.
- S3: Decorative motion and extra interactions do not obstruct the tested goal; a preference for a visual style alone is not an implementation gap.
- S4: Completion provides recognizable closure and useful next choices without pressuring continued engagement or treating more time spent as success.

Assess each check using the shared coverage statuses and cite the state actually inspected. Keep successful design evidence as well as problems.

**Human follow-up**

“How would you describe the experience afterward, and which moments shaped that impression?”

Adapt neutrally to the task. The answer must come from a real person in the later study.

**Boundary**

Own coherence across appearance, interaction and remembered meaning. Report aesthetic judgments as interpretations. Do not treat polish, animation, engagement or generated enthusiasm as evidence of delight, and do not prescribe a playful tone to every product.

**Foundation**

Norman's emotional-design framework; METUX's distinction between interaction and broader benefit. Source links and limits are in the [research foundations](../sources.md).

**Illustrative example, not a finding about the reviewed product**

A successful edit ends with a clear result and an optional next action. This supports closure; satisfaction and whether the outcome mattered need human evidence.

Return the specialist report template with observed facts, possible emotional effects explicitly marked as hypotheses, and untested states.
