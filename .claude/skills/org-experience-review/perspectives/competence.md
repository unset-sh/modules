**Competence and progress reviewer**

Guiding question: **“Can I accomplish what I came to do?”**

Read the [shared instructions](../shared-instructions.md) and [report template](../report-template.md) before using this perspective. Receive only the completed run brief and your assigned materials. Treat the question as a review lens; never answer it as a fabricated human emotional report.

**Task**

Attempt one meaningful goal from the supplied starting state, including an authorized correctable input mistake.

**Observable checks**

- C1: Required steps have an identifiable role in the stated goal; record unexplained prerequisites rather than assuming fewer steps are always better.
- C2: Input requirements are discoverable before avoidable errors occur.
- C3: Feedback identifies how to correct the tested mistake and preserves valid work where appropriate.
- C4: Completion is distinguishable from an intermediate state, and the result can be inspected.

Assess each check using the shared coverage statuses and cite the state actually inspected. Keep successful design evidence as well as problems.

**Human follow-up**

“Which parts helped you make progress, and where did you get stuck?”

Adapt neutrally to the task. The answer must come from a real person in the later study.

**Boundary**

Own support for effective action. Autonomy owns choice and reversibility. Do not infer confidence from task completion, speed, badges, praise or the model's ability to overcome an obstacle.

**Foundation**

Self-determination theory's competence need; Nielsen's error prevention and recovery guidance. Source links and limits are in the [research foundations](../sources.md).

**Illustrative example, not a finding about the reviewed product**

After an invalid field is corrected, previously entered content remains intact. This supports recovery; whether it increases confidence requires human testing.

Return the specialist report template with observed facts, possible emotional effects explicitly marked as hypotheses, and untested states.
